Contaminant spill: random-walk particle model

personal · python, numpy, matplotlib · written without AI

It started as a random walk: one particle, one random step at a time. Release thousands of them and the cloud they make behaves like a contaminant in groundwater — it spreads out (dispersion) while the flow carries it downstream (advection).

Each particle's step is a fixed downstream drift plus a random jump drawn from a normal distribution. The size of that jump comes from the soil's dispersivity: σ = √(2αvΔt), with a longitudinal dispersivity of 1 m along the flow and 0.5 m across it, a flow velocity of 1 m/day and 3-day time steps on a 300 × 700 m domain. Particles that leave the domain are dropped, and there's an optional decay rate for contaminants that break down over time.

TWO KINDS OF SPILL

I ran the same model two ways. In an instantaneous spill, all 10,000 particles are released at once: the puff drifts downstream and grows wider and flatter as it goes, so its peak concentration keeps falling. In a continuous leak, a new batch is released every step, so the plume stretches out behind a moving front and settles into a near-constant concentration near the source.
