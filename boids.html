Simulating a flock from three simple rules

personal · python, pygame · written without AI

OBJECTIVE

Boids is an example of simulated swarm intelligence, it revolves around the idea of assigning simple rules of interaction to many agents and allowing them to interact. Under this ruleset it simulates the flocking behaviour of birds, fish, and other related group motion. Boids was developed by Craig Reynolds in 1986.

WORK

  • Built the simulation in Python (pygame) from three local rules:
    • Alignment: steer toward the average heading of neighbours.
    • Cohesion: drift toward the average position of neighbours.
    • Separation: push away from anything too close.
  • Capped how fast each boid can turn and fixed its speed, which stops the flock from collapsing into one point or flying apart.

RESULTS

  • Whole flocks form, turn together and split apart from the three rules alone.
  • My goal with this project, and the reason behind not using AI, was to improve my proficiency in Python, which I succeeded in.
  • After finishing the project I used Claude to port the same rules to JavaScript as the flock of fish behind this site, steering around text and images.

WHAT I LEARNED

  • Boids was my first personal project in Python after completing the course I was following.
  • The major challenges were making the code efficient and getting the interactions between boids to work properly. As this project is non-definite, as it was hard to tell whether it was working as intended or not.
