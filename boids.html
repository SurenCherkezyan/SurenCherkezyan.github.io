Boids

personal · python, pygame · written without AI

Boids are a classic model of flocking: no leader and no plan, just each bird reacting to the neighbours it can see. Three local rules are enough for whole flocks to form, turn and split.

  • Alignment — steer toward the average heading of neighbours within 25 px (strength 0.05).
  • Cohesion — drift toward the average position of neighbours within 120 px (strength 0.004).
  • Separation — push away from anything closer than 20 px, harder the closer it gets (1/d², strength 10).

Each boid only sees what's within 120° of its heading, so it ignores the ones directly behind it. The combined steering is capped at 0.25 per frame and every boid keeps a fixed speed of 4, which stops the flock from collapsing into one point or flying apart.

The flock behind these pages is a JavaScript port of the same rules, slowed down, with one addition: instead of wrapping around the screen, boids are drawn as small fish and turn away from the edges and from blocks of text and images, the same way they avoid each other.
